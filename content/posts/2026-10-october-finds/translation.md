## 01 / A little more colour

*PEOPLE & PERSONAL STYLE*

### Peggy Gou.

I've followed Peggy Gou for ages, but only recently found out she studied at LCF, part of UAL. Somehow I had no idea until now.

What I love about her outfits is how the individual pieces clash a little when she puts them together. A bright red suit, a white shirt, a black tie, then red-framed glasses on top. Even the tie isn't neatly tucked away. The red is already loud, and she adds more red. The all-pink look takes a different approach: pink shirt, pink hat, pink oversized bag. Similar colours, different textures and shapes. It doesn't feel sickly at all.

Then there's the flash of bright green under a purple jacket, or blue hair with a blue bag. She knows exactly where to add a little more. These photos make me want to wear colour. They make me feel that I could dress with that much energy today, too.

### YELLOW does more than wear clothes.

![YELLOW wearing an apricot-yellow suit jacket over a polka-dot pleated skirt, from his HEAVEN RAVEN interview](yellow-heaven-raven-dress.jpg "HEAVEN RAVEN, 2022 / Dries Van Noten jacket, H&M skirt")

![YELLOW wearing a black-and-white sports top with exaggerated leather sleeves, photographed for Vogue Taiwan](yellow-vogue-leather-sleeves.jpg "Vogue Taiwan, 2023 / PUMA T7 top, JUST IN XX leather sleeves")

YELLOW has such a distinctive way of dressing, too. With this apricot-yellow suit jacket, he isn't wearing a conventional pair of trousers, but an enormous polka-dot pleated skirt. Up top, there are the jacket's shoulders and lapels; below, everything loosens and spreads out. Structure and softness layered together. As if that huge skirt weren't enough, he's holding a little red violin. The whole photograph is so exaggerated.

In the other look, he's wearing a black-and-white sports top with leather sleeves over the top's own sleeves. His arms suddenly balloon out, changing the sportswear's clean silhouette. A suit jacket with a huge skirt, a sports top with leather sleeves. They're all familiar clothes, but he's so good at making them look different together.

I'd wanted to see YELLOW at Mañana 明日公园 in Haikou, but forgot to reserve a ticket.

## 02 / Field notebooks. I bought ten.

*THINK OF THINGS / COPY CORNER LABEL PRODUCTS*

![The six THINK OF THINGS RISO survey field notebook covers together](think-of-things-six-covers.jpg "The six cover designs in the collection. Buying ten notebooks does not mean ten different designs.")

*RISO FIELD NOTEBOOKS*

10 notebooks

I really love this batch of field notebooks from THINK OF THINGS. I bought ten.

They're from COPY CORNER LABEL PRODUCTS' RISO series: six creators, six covers. Given the same narrow rectangle, one plays with the grid, another draws ants, and another simply asks you to close the notebook and take a break. Every one is worth a look.

![Official cover image of 藤田紗衣's 3×3](riso-01-fujita-3x3.jpg)

### 01 / 藤田紗衣 · 3×3

Starting with the 3 mm grid on the inside pages, the design draws with question marks, exclamation marks and tildes. The tiny symbols gather and disperse, leaving broad areas of blank space to emerge between them, while the background shifts from pale green to pink. It's very subtle, but there's plenty to look at closely. I love seeing the most ordinary punctuation used like this.

![Official cover image of nico ito's Viral Trap](riso-02-nico-ito-viral-trap.jpg)

### 02 / nico ito · Viral Trap

A bright green background, a purple-and-white striped lint roller, ants stuck to it, and one ant circled and enlarged. Everything is familiar, but put together it's so odd, like a little ad for something you can't quite identify. Just looking at the cover makes me wonder what will be written inside.

![Official cover image of 田中せり's Rest Field Notebook (休憩野帳)](riso-03-tanaka-rest.jpg)

### 03 / 田中せり · Rest Field Notebook (休憩野帳)

Pink, yellow, blue and purple bleed into one another. It turns out the design grew out of a hand acupressure chart, with ten gradient-printing layers overlaid. Because the notebook fits neatly in the palm, she wanted people at work to close it and take care of their bodies for a moment. I like how faintly the outline of the hand is hidden in there, while she's so generous with the colour.

![Official cover image of 鳳崎優和's RISO TARTAN CHECK](riso-04-hosaki-tartan.jpg)

### 04 / 鳳崎優和 · RISO TARTAN CHECK

Fine red lines run through a purple, green and blue check. The slight misregistration of the RISO colours makes the orderly pattern wobble a little. The creator took the notebook cover's original linen-textured paper as a starting point for the tartan, wanting people to carry it like a fashion accessory. I'd love to see these colours on clothes, too.

![Official cover image of 中村至男's Forest of Grids (方眼の森)](riso-05-nakamura-grid-forest.jpg)

### 05 / 中村至男 · Forest of Grids (方眼の森)

A little blue child, with a break in the curve of the head and a body that seems assembled from separate parts. There's a very faint grid behind it. This one striking blue is all the picture needs. The creator describes a child entering a forest of grids, where scattered thoughts gradually connect into a network. Looking at it makes scribbling and doodling feel entirely reasonable.

![Official cover image of 田中良治's Taro & Jiro](riso-06-tanaka-taro-jiro.jpg)

### 06 / 田中良治 · Taro & Jiro

Little black squares form two dogs, outlined in purple. They're Taro and Jiro, who were found alive in Antarctica in 1959, the same year the survey field notebook was born. A purple 1959 is printed on the back. He connects those two events and distils them into such simple shapes. Knowing that makes the cover even better.

The six look good together, too. The pale ones are really pale; the bright ones are really bright. They don't all need to look like members of the same family.

## 03 / I don't ski. I wear them anyway.

*SMALL THINGS / GOOD DESIGN*

![686 long socks with a black-and-green mountain design, image supplied by Lucille](686-socks-green-mountains.png)

![686 black-and-white checkerboard long socks, image supplied by Lucille](686-socks-checkerboard.png "686 long socks / Images supplied by Lucille: black-and-green mountains and black-and-white checkerboard")

### Snowy mountains race down from the cuff.

I'm very happy with the long socks I bought from 686, too. Against big blocks of green and black, white snowy mountains race straight down from the cuff, with grey or olive green finishing the toes and heels. Big graphics, clear shapes. Even the socks look this good. I don't ski, but I wear them anyway. Comfortable and good-looking. The black-and-white checkerboard pair is great, too. Such a direct graphic.

## 06 / One last thing: a good-looking website

*ONE MORE GOOD THING*

### twomuch.studio ↗

![Cyan racing car and magenta visuals created by Twomuch Studio for the PP Model typeface](twomuch-pp-model.jpg "PP Model / Twomuch Studio × Pangram Pangram Foundry")

One last website that looks really good: twomuch.studio.

The PP Model series has a black background, a bright cyan racing car, magenta light, and type broken apart like model-kit pieces. These are launch visuals for a typeface, and even the model car's injection-moulded sprues, stickers and instructions become design cues. I like this approach: you can keep taking an idea further, and there's still more to play with as it moves from one image to another.

[Visit twomuch.studio ↗](https://www.twomuch.studio/)

![Actual screenshot of the current Studio Nari official website](studio-nari-homepage-2026-10-27.jpg "Studio Nari / Screenshot of the current official website, 2026.10.27. Website design: Twomuch Studio; the work shown belongs to its respective creators.")

![Actual screenshot of the current Daisy Chain official website](daisy-chain-homepage-2026-10-27.jpg "Daisy Chain / Screenshot of the current official website, 2026.10.27. Website design: Twomuch Studio; the work shown belongs to its respective creators.")

Its website for Studio Nari, meanwhile, is very sparse. Images of different sizes sit far apart, white space flowing between them, letting your eyes jump from one block to another. Daisy Chain packs its thumbnails into one long strip at the top, then gives the whole screen below over to the work. A thin navigation bar against an enormous image: the proportions feel so satisfying. The same studio, completely different layouts. Both are worth clicking through.
