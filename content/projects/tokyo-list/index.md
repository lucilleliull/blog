## The premise

The best travel lists are personal. Sun and I had collected more than a hundred Tokyo places across two Google Maps lists—shops, galleries, cafés, restaurants, and spaces chosen for atmosphere as much as utility. The knowledge was useful, but the interface was not.

## The problem

A dense cloud of saved pins does not reveal taste. It cannot explain what deserves a detour, help someone browse by neighbourhood, or turn a long list into an itinerary. I wanted the collection to feel edited: clear enough to use on the street and considered enough to share.

## The approach

I gave an AI collaborator the two source lists, a visual reference, and a precise description of the feeling I wanted. Together we shaped the information architecture, cleaned the Tokyo-only data, added ratings, and iterated on the details—from the quiet washi palette to clickable overview statistics.

## The outcome

The result is a responsive field guide to 105 places across 20 categories. Visitors can search, filter by category or area, browse a focused Top Picks list, and open any place directly in Google Maps. What began as private saved data became a small, durable public object.
